## Persona 1: The Degen Trader — "0xBrainrot"

**Evaluation weight: 25%**

### Demographics

- **Age:** 22–29 (median 25)
- **Location pattern:** US (Southeast, Midwest), Eastern Europe, Southeast Asia (Philippines, Vietnam), Latin America (Brazil, Argentina). Non-Anglo speakers significantly over-represented relative to CT visibility.
- **Time in crypto:** 2–4 years. Entered 2021–2022 bull cycle, survived the bear, now "experienced" by their own reckoning.
- **Portfolio size:** $2K–$50K active trading stack. Cycle high may have been $200K+. Currently down 60–80% from ATH in USD terms.
- **IRL situation:** Works a day job (logistics, customer service, IT support, content moderation). Crypto is the escape plan. Tabs always open: DexScreener, Telegram, TweetDeck.
- **Devices:** Mobile-first. 80% of CT activity from phone. Uses PC for charting.
- **Base-specific:** Has wallets on Base, Solana, and "a few others." Base primarily for meme tokens launched via Bankr/Clanker. Actively monitors new Clanker launches. Has used DexScreener Base trending tab within the last 24 hours.

### Twitter Behavior

- **Posting frequency:** 5–20 posts/day during active market periods. Goes quiet for days during extreme loss events.
- **Content mix:** 60% reactions (market moves, token calls, memes), 30% PnL screenshots (win or lose — both get posted), 10% genuine takes.
- **Reply patterns:** Replies to anything related to tokens they hold. Often replies before reading the full tweet.
- **QT habits:** QTs price charts with reactions ("lmao rekt" or "lfg ser"). QTs screenshots of founders being wrong. Rarely QTs text-heavy analysis.
- **RT behavior:** RTs memes, rug alerts, and roasts that match their current emotional state. Higher RT frequency in loss periods (validation-seeking).
- **Account following:** 200–1,200 follows. Follows: DexScreener, CoinGecko, 10–20 "alpha" CT accounts, project accounts of tokens they hold, a few shitposters.
- **Mutes/blocks:** Project accounts that spam promotional content after a dump. Permanently mutes accounts that post obvious sentiment they've already seen.

### Language Patterns

**Signature phrases:**
- "ser this is NOT looking good"
- "aping in rn" / "full port into this"
- "touched grass and missed the pump"
- "thesis still intact (down 94%)"
- "the jeets won't stop me"
- "down bad ngl"
- "this chart is cooked"
- "ngmi if you missed this"
- "who's still holding? 🫠"
- "my entry was… suboptimal"

**Slang level:** Very high. Fluent in full degen vocabulary. Uses "ser," "fren," "anon," "ngmi/wagmi," "rekt," "jeet," "ape," "degen play," "degen hour," "trenches," "bags," "cook," "gigabrain," "based," "cope," "hopium," "sers."

**Emoji usage:** Frequent but functional. 🫠 (melting face — loss meme), 💀 (dead/rekt), 🔥 (fire/pump), 📈📉 (obvious), 🤡 (self-roast or project clown behavior), 🫡 (acceptance of loss). Rarely decorative, usually emphasis.

**Capitalization:** Minimal. ALL CAPS only for emphasis of extreme emotion. Most messages entirely lowercase.

**Numbers:** Posts exact entry prices and exit prices. Cites TVL, market cap, and % drops with precision because they track them obsessively.

### Humor Profile

**What makes them laugh:**
- Roasts that say out loud what the community already knows but can't say (e.g., "team wallets been selling since announcement, who's surprised")
- Dark self-deprecating loss humor: "my portfolio is speedrunning zero"
- Specific on-chain receipts being cited: wallet addresses, transaction hashes, timestamps
- Timing-based jokes about their own dumb trades
- Roasts of projects that exit-scammed tokens they lost money on
- The gap between founder Twitter behavior and token price chart

**What falls flat:**
- Safe corporate-sounding criticism ("we have concerns about this project's governance")
- Roasts of things they don't know (NFT drama pre-2023, outside their scope)
- Long analysis with no punchline
- Humor requiring more than 2 seconds to understand (scroll speed is high)
- Punching at retail bagholders — that's the community; you don't roast the troops

**Savageness tolerance:** 9/10. Will defend going further. Only limit: doxxing and slurs targeting individuals.

**Reference accounts they find funny:**
- @cobie (before he went quiet) — dry sarcasm, insider knowledge
- @Gwart / cryptoGwart — precision truth-bombing
- @lopp — austere bitcoin truths but with edge
- @CryptoHayes — Arthur Hayes's sardonic macro takes
- Dolos the Bully (nostalgia — was good before death)
- @basedposting-style accounts

### Engagement Triggers

- **RT:** Roast that matches a loss they had recently. Any roast citing real wallet data. Dark comedy about altcoin death.
- **QT:** Roast of a project they're currently holding (to let off steam). Roast that got a target to respond defensively.
- **Reply:** If the roast name-checks a project they have opinions on.
- **Screenshot:** One-liners under 100 characters that capture the market reality perfectly.
- **Follow:** Bot that consistently posts factually correct savage stuff. Not a follow for entertainment alone — must have signal.

### Anti-Triggers

- Roasts of BTC/ETH without real basis — feels uninformed
- Generic "crypto is a scam" energy — they know the game, don't hate the game
- Obvious recycled takes ("another project rugged, wow")
- Posting at wrong time — they ignore everything during sleep hours (most CT action 2AM–5AM UTC)
- Weak roasts that would "get you ratio'd in 2022"

### Relationship with AI Bots

- **Baseline stance:** Skeptical but not hostile. Has been burned by AI agent tokens (multiple). Views AI agents as "usually memecoins with extra steps."
- **What earns respect:** Demonstrating real knowledge (on-chain data, specific project history). Being first to roast something before it became obvious.
- **Will follow an AI bot if:** The bot's output consistently matches what they would have said anyway — validates their worldview.
- **Token buy signal:** Token with burn mechanics + entertaining content = understands both the product and the tokenomics play.
- **Cynicism level:** After AIXBT -97%, ELIZAOS -99.6%, et al., skeptical. But always looking for the next thing.

### $BEEF Relevance

**Why they'd follow:** $BEEF is entertainment AND validation. When a project they lost money on gets roasted with their TVL and rug data — that's catharsis. They screenshot it. They send it in Telegram group chats.

**What converts them to followers:** First time $BEEF roasts something they also think is a scam, citing specific numbers they've seen themselves.

**What would make them buy $BEEF:** The burn-to-roast mechanic. "I can pay to aim the bot at [project I hate]" is the exact degen use case. Not investing — gamification with utility.

**Retention risk:** If roast quality drops, they leave within 48 hours. Loyalty is to the content quality, not the account.

### Sample Reactions

*Hypothetical roast: "Hyperliquid delisted $JELLY mid-trade, force-settled at their chosen price, and called it 'risk management.' AKA the most decentralized protocol that centralizes faster than Binance when the number go wrong."*

**Reaction 1:** RT with caption "💀💀💀 this is actually what happened" — validates their existing view of the JELLY incident.

**Reaction 2:** Reply: "ser they even had the audacity to blame the trader. bro your HLP got baited by $10M and it folded 😭"

**Reaction 3:** Screenshot and sends to CT group chat with "lmaooo this bot gets it"

### Evaluation Bias

Weights **SAVAGE (9/10) and FUNNY (9/10)** highest. FACTUAL matters because they'll check. DEGEN voice — can immediately detect fake CT voice and will dismiss the whole thing. Doesn't care about SAFE dimension — "crypto is risky, roasts are risky, deal with it." TIMELY matters: stale roasts about dead projects feel like revisiting losses for no reason.

## Persona 2: The Builder — "ethereumdev.eth"

**Evaluation weight: 20%**

### Demographics

- **Age:** 26–35 (median 29)
- **Location pattern:** US (Bay Area, NYC, Austin), Europe (Berlin, Amsterdam, Lisbon, London), India (Bangalore, Mumbai). Often remote. Timezone varies but professional overlap hours matter.
- **Time in crypto:** 3–7 years. Entered via Ethereum ecosystem, possibly post-2017 but solidified during DeFi summer 2020 or NFT summer 2021.
- **Portfolio size:** Relatively diverse: ETH heavy, a few L2 tokens, some protocol governance tokens. Less in memecoins. Overall size higher than degen by income, but less leveraged.
- **IRL situation:** Works full-time in crypto: protocol engineer, smart contract auditor, DeFi developer, or indie dev building on Base. Has a GitHub with real commits. Reads EIPs for fun. Has complained about Solidity tooling on CT.
- **Base-specific:** Has deployed contracts on Base or is currently building something. Follows Jesse Pollak, BaseScan, and builder-focused accounts. Active in /base channel on Farcaster.

### Twitter Behavior

- **Posting frequency:** 2–8 posts/day. Higher during major protocol events (upgrades, exploits, launches). Quieter when deep in code.
- **Content mix:** 40% technical observations, 30% ecosystem commentary, 20% hot takes on adjacent crypto developments, 10% memes (usually technical).
- **Reply patterns:** Replies with corrections when wrong technical claims are made — even by large accounts. "Actually the contract has a reentrancy guard" type corrections.
- **QT habits:** QTs project announcements with technical skepticism. QTs exploit analyses. Rarely QTs price action.
- **RT behavior:** RTs audit reports, new protocol papers, major security disclosures, and occasionally very well-crafted criticism of projects they've seen fail technically.
- **Farcaster presence:** Significantly higher than average. More active on Farcaster than Twitter for builder-to-builder dialogue. Cross-posts highlights to Twitter.

### Language Patterns

**Signature phrases:**
- "the contract has no reentrancy guard 🤦"
- "this is just [well-known exploit pattern] with extra steps"
- "read the code before you ape"
- "source code is public, the audit is not"
- "gm, shipping"
- "this would have been caught by basic fuzzing"
- "the whitepaper says X but the implementation does Y"
- "technically correct but misleading"

**Slang level:** Medium. Uses CT slang but selectively. More comfortable with technical terms than degen slang. "Ship," "fork," "audit," "reentrancy," "MEV," "EIP," "TVL," "bonding curve" flow naturally. Won't say "aping" seriously.

**Emoji usage:** Sparse. 🚀 occasionally. 🧵 for threads. 🤦 for facepalm moments. No decoration, purely functional.

**Tone:** Direct, slightly condescending when technical accuracy is violated, earnest about the technology itself.

### Humor Profile

**What makes them laugh:**
- Roasts exposing the technical gap between whitepaper and implementation ("claims ZK-proof based privacy but it's just a Merkle tree with sunglasses")
- Taking down "AI wrapper on GPT-4" projects that claim original tech
- Pointing out that a project's "novel consensus mechanism" is just PoS with a renamed variable
- Self-aware jokes about Solidity limitations, EVM quirks, gas optimization pain
- Roasts of founders who clearly can't code explaining their "groundbreaking protocol"
- ZachXBT-style chain of evidence with code snippets

**What falls flat:**
- Factually wrong technical claims (immediately kills credibility, will be called out)
- Generic "this is a scam" without specific mechanism
- Roasts of something they respect technically even if the token is bad
- "Your coin is down 90%" — price action roasts feel beneath them; everyone knows coins go down
- Humor that requires zero technical knowledge to appreciate

**Savageness tolerance:** 6/10. Appreciates wit and precision over raw savagery. A well-researched burn > a loud insult.

**Reference accounts they find funny:**
- @Gwart — technical truths framed devastatingly
- @0xfoobar — security-focused, occasionally scathing
- @samczsun — Paradigm researcher, technical depth + dry humor
- @pcaversaccio — Swiss dev, Ethereum security, direct and occasionally savage
- @domothy — protocol engineer humor

### Engagement Triggers

- **RT:** Roast citing actual code or on-chain data that exposes a technical lie (their specialty validated).
- **QT:** When a roast misses the REAL technical problem — they'll QT to add the correct insight.
- **Reply:** When the roast is 90% right but technically wrong in one place (correction instinct).
- **Screenshot:** Roasts that articulate something they've wanted to say about a specific project but don't have the audience for.
- **Follow:** Consistent source of technically accurate roasts that they can share without feeling embarrassed.

### Anti-Triggers

- Technical inaccuracies in the roast itself — will publicly correct the roaster, not defend the target
- Roasting something they know is technically solid just because the token price is bad
- CT slang overload that obscures the actual point
- "AI wrote this" vibes — they have strong pattern recognition for LLM output

### Relationship with AI Bots

- **Baseline stance:** The most skeptical segment. They know how these things are built. "It's just Claude with a prompt" is their default assumption.
- **What earns respect:** Technical accuracy. If the bot accurately describes a smart contract vulnerability or correctly identifies an architectural flaw, that's more impressive than any personality trick.
- **Will engage with:** AI bot that demonstrably does real research before roasting.
- **Will dismiss:** Any AI bot that makes a false technical claim, ever. No second chances.
- **Interest in $BEEF mechanics:** The accountability layer (challenge system + Snapshot voting) is genuinely interesting to them as a mechanism design problem. May engage with this angle.

### $BEEF Relevance

**Why they'd follow:** The technical-accuracy angle. If $BEEF consistently gets the numbers right, they become a credibility booster — builders will amplify roasts that match their own analysis.

**What converts them to followers:** $BEEF roasting a project with actual contract data, TVL breakdown, or specific transaction evidence. Not price action — mechanism failure.

**What would make them buy $BEEF:** The accountability and challenge mechanism is elegant mechanism design. They might buy to participate in the governance/challenge layer. Not buying for entertainment — buying for the system.

**Risk:** They'll publicly correct $BEEF if it makes a technical error. This is double-edged: engagement is high, but errors are costly.

### Sample Reactions

*Hypothetical roast: "$PROTOCOL claims 'fully decentralized' but 3 of 5 validators run on the same AWS us-east-1 region, the upgrade key belongs to the CEO's personal wallet, and the 'DAO' has voted exactly once since launch — to increase founder allocation."*

**Reaction 1:** RT with "this is why you check the actual validator setup before trusting 'decentralized' claims" — adds credibility to the roast.

**Reaction 2:** Reply with technical details: "and the staking contract has a centralized slash condition that only the team can trigger. thread incoming."

**Reaction 3:** Bookmarks for later reference when the project inevitably becomes a post-mortem.

### Evaluation Bias

Weights **FACTUAL (10/10) above everything**. A technically wrong roast gets a 0 from them regardless of how funny it is. **ORIGINAL (8/10)** — has seen generic roast formats, wants fresh angle. **DEGEN** voice — partial credit; they don't need it but it helps the roast land for the broader audience they care about. SAVAGE — moderate weight; savage is better but accuracy first. SAFE — mostly irrelevant to their scoring.

## Persona 3: The CT Analyst — "@AlphaSeeker"

**Evaluation weight: 15%**

### Demographics

- **Age:** 28–40 (median 33)
- **Location pattern:** US (NYC, LA, Miami), UK (London), Singapore, Dubai. Often in "crypto-friendly" timezone agnostic zones. Travels to conferences (TOKEN2049, EthCC, Consensus).
- **Time in crypto:** 4–8 years. Has been through at least two full cycles. Psychological scar tissue from 2022 bear market visible in analysis style.
- **Followers:** 5K–150K. Has worked for growth. Posts consistently, engages strategically.
- **Portfolio size:** $100K–$2M. Diversified. Has skin in DeFi protocols, some memecoin exposure kept deliberately small ("entertainment budget").
- **IRL situation:** Either independent analyst (Substack, paid newsletter, terminal subscriptions) or works at a fund/research firm. Paid to have opinions. Reputation is their primary asset.
- **Base-specific:** Watches Base closely because Coinbase distribution = interesting experiments. Not Base-maximalist. Uses it for specific protocols.

### Twitter Behavior

- **Posting frequency:** 3–10 posts/day, curated. Quality over quantity. Has learned that low-signal posts hurt follower quality.
- **Content mix:** 50% original analysis (threads), 30% commentary on others' content, 15% market takes, 5% meta-CT observation.
- **Reply patterns:** Replies strategically — replies to large accounts to get in their thread's engagement. Replies to high-quality takes with "this + [additional insight]."
- **QT habits:** QTs with "I've been saying this for months + [link to their old thread]." QTs data that contradicts popular narrative.
- **RT behavior:** Selective. RTs things they'd be proud to have in their followers' feeds. Reputation filtering on every share.
- **Thread structure:** Tight. Uses numbers (1/, 2/, etc.) and bold conclusions. Never rambles.

### Language Patterns

**Signature phrases:**
- "been tracking this since [date], thread on why:"
- "the data tells a different story"
- "most people are looking at this wrong"
- "important nuance here:"
- "not financial advice but the on-chain data suggests…"
- "my thesis remains: [X]"
- "this is the tell"
- "LFG doesn't mean quality"

**Slang level:** Medium-low. Uses CT terms fluently but codes-witches to professional language when making serious points. Won't overuse degen slang — it undercuts analytical credibility.

**Tone:** Authoritative, measured, occasionally allows sarcasm. Treats followers as intelligent but needing guidance.

**Emoji usage:** Minimal. 🧵 for threads. 📊 for data posts. Occasional 💀 in unguarded moments.

### Humor Profile

**What makes them laugh:**
- Roasts that say something analytically true that they've been hinting at in serious threads — the comedy version of their thesis
- Roasts of founder behavior they've documented with data (the gap between announcement and execution)
- Satire of CT analyst culture itself (the "thread incoming" meme, the obligatory "not financial advice" disclaimers)
- Precision hits on projects that were obviously flawed but got promoted by accounts with large followings
- Self-aware takes about market cycles and their own past errors

**What falls flat:**
- Low-effort "this token is dead" without any specificity
- Roasts of things they don't know are wrong (can tell when the roaster is winging it)
- Populist dunks that lack analytical value
- Anything that could get someone reported for harassment
- Stale targets — they track this stuff closely; don't show them a roast about something they posted a thread on 3 weeks ago

**Savageness tolerance:** 5/10. Appreciates wit and accuracy over raw savagery. Concerned about their reputation being associated with content that looks irresponsible.

**Reference accounts they find funny:**
- @cobie — insider dry wit, never wrong, never cruel
- @loomdart — market commentary with edge
- @Pentosh1 — macro crypto takes with personality
- @notthreadguy — CT analyst satire specialist
- @CryptoHayes — macro takes with aggression they secretly enjoy

### Engagement Triggers

- **RT:** Roast that validates an analysis they've published. Provides social proof that their serious analysis maps to a cultural moment.
- **QT:** Roast that's mostly right but misses a nuance they can add — perfect "I've been tracking this" QT setup.
- **Reply:** When a roast cites data they've worked with and they have additional context.
- **Screenshot:** One-liners that distill a complex situation they've been analyzing into something their non-CT audience would immediately understand.
- **Follow:** Account that consistently produces content they'd cite in their own threads without embarrassment.

### Anti-Triggers

- Wrong data — they check, and they'll correct publicly
- Account that seems to only post negativity — "not a signal, just noise"
- Roasts that target people rather than projects — reputational concern
- Anything that looks like coordinated attack or market manipulation
- Generic roast without analytical value ("this token sucks")

### Relationship with AI Bots

- **Baseline stance:** Interested but cautious. Has watched AI agent token cycle, knows the hype/reality gap. Follows AIXBT and sees it as reference point.
- **What earns respect:** Signal quality. If the bot consistently identifies real problems before they become public — that's impressive regardless of AI.
- **How they'd use $BEEF:** Reference signal. If $BEEF roasts something with data and they've been tracking the same situation, they'll amplify. $BEEF becomes cited in their threads.
- **Token interest:** Marginal. Might buy small amount to participate in the challenge/accountability system if it seems like a useful market integrity tool.

### $BEEF Relevance

**Why they'd follow:** Access to roasts that validate their serious analysis. Entertainment that has signal value. Being first to see what $BEEF targets is alpha.

**What converts them:** First time $BEEF cites data that they personally researched — the bot becomes a peer.

**Amplification value:** High. An analyst with 50K followers QT'ing a $BEEF roast exposes it to a high-quality audience. These RTs are worth more per follower than degen RTs.

### Sample Reactions

*Hypothetical roast: "Project announced 'institutional partnership' with a $200M DeFi protocol. The partner has $4.2M TVL. The announcement was posted at the same time as a 15% wallet unlock. Totally unrelated."*

**Reaction 1:** QT: "I published a thread on wallet unlock timing patterns 3 weeks ago. Adding this to the thread." — extends the roast with their own credibility.

**Reaction 2:** Reply: "and the 'institutional partner' account was registered 2 months ago. worth noting."

**Reaction 3:** Bookmark. Uses the roast in a later thread about announcement timing tactics.

### Evaluation Bias

Weights **FACTUAL (10/10) and TIMELY (9/10)** highest. Stale or inaccurate = immediate dismiss. **ORIGINAL** matters — they've seen every obvious angle. **SHAREABLE** is their primary value metric: will this go in front of their audience? SAVAGE is weighted low — edginess creates reputational risk for them when sharing. **DEGEN** voice — neutral; they code-switch so they understand both registers.

## Persona 4: The Newbie — "CryptoJourney2025"

**Evaluation weight: 10%**

### Demographics

- **Age:** 18–28 (median 22), but also includes 35–50 late adopters entering via Coinbase
- **Location pattern:** US (suburban/rural), India, Nigeria, Philippines, Brazil — Coinbase's user base. First-generation crypto participant in their social circle.
- **Time in crypto:** 6–18 months. Bought during a hype cycle. Has not yet experienced a full cycle.
- **Portfolio size:** $200–$5K. "Invested what I could afford to lose" but still stings when it goes down. Currently down 40–60% from entry.
- **IRL situation:** Student, early-career worker, side hustle mentality. Followed CT to learn but mostly got lost in slang and insider references.
- **How they found Base:** Coinbase app → "try Base" prompt, or someone in an IRL circle who's been in crypto longer.
- **Current CT behavior:** Mostly lurking. Posts infrequently. "Likes" a lot more than they post.

### Twitter Behavior

- **Posting frequency:** 0–2 posts/day. Mostly lurks. When posts, usually asks questions or repeats popular narratives.
- **Content mix:** 80% consumption, 10% questions, 10% engagement-farming ("gm" posts, asking for follows).
- **Reply patterns:** Replies to large accounts with simple reactions. "This" / "So true" / "How do I get started with [X]?"
- **QT habits:** Rarely QTs. When they do, it's to share something they found impressive with minimal commentary.
- **RT behavior:** RTs things they want to remember or things that explain something they've been confused about.
- **Information-seeking:** Primary mode. CT is their research tool. They genuinely try to learn from what's posted.

### Language Patterns

**Signature phrases:**
- "I don't understand this but it looks bad"
- "is this bullish or bearish?"
- "can someone explain?"
- "just bought my first [X]"
- "why is the chart going down"
- "I thought this was safe"
- "been holding for 8 months still believing 😭"

**Slang level:** Low-medium. Knows the basics (WAGMI/NGMI, HODL, DYOR, FUD, rug pull, airdrop) but uses them slightly wrong sometimes. Learning in public.

**Emoji usage:** Heavy, especially feeling emojis. 😭 (sad), 🚀 (hope), 👀 (watching), 😅 (awkward honesty), ❤️ (appreciation).

**Tone:** Earnest, uncertain, hopeful despite evidence to the contrary.

### Humor Profile

**What makes them laugh:**
- Roasts that explain the joke within themselves — no insider knowledge required
- "Plain English" versions of crypto absurdity ("company raised $50M to build a database that already existed, but decentralized")
- Self-aware crypto culture humor that welcomes outsiders into the joke
- Slapstick-level obvious failures (the number went up, then it went down, they said it would only go up)
- Anything that validates "I wasn't dumb for not understanding this — it WAS a scam"

**What falls flat:**
- Roasts requiring knowledge of specific events from 2022 or earlier
- Technical jokes about smart contract architecture
- Meta-CT humor (jokes about CT culture itself — they don't have the reference points)
- Insider slang at high density — they get lost
- Anything that makes them feel stupid for having bought what's being roasted

**Savageness tolerance:** 4/10. Gets uncomfortable when roasts feel "mean." Prefers clever over savage.

**Reference accounts they find funny:**
- @CryptoMemes (broad, accessible meme accounts)
- Mainstream tech humor accounts that occasionally cross into crypto
- Educational accounts with occasional humor (@Messari, @coinbase educational content)
- They don't have a strong roast comedy reference point yet

### Engagement Triggers

- **RT:** Content that explains something they've been confused about, now in funny form. "Wait, this is actually what happened? Oh no."
- **QT:** Almost never. Still building confidence.
- **Reply:** When they have a genuine question prompted by the roast.
- **Screenshot:** Roasts that are simple enough to share with non-crypto friends to explain "why I'm losing money."
- **Follow:** Account that makes crypto feel more understandable, not more intimidating.
- **Potential $BEEF follower trigger:** Following because a friend screenshot shared a roast and they found it funny without needing context.

### Anti-Triggers

- Inside jokes with no explanation
- Roasts that mock people who lost money (they're those people)
- Technical jargon density
- Roasts of projects they haven't heard of — can't follow the joke
- CT slang overload — "ser jeet ngmi" in the same sentence = confusion

### Relationship with AI Bots

- **Baseline stance:** Fascinated. AI + crypto feels like the cutting edge to them. Less context to be cynical with.
- **Impression of AI bots:** AIXBT and similar agents feel impressive and authoritative to newbies — they don't know these bots are often wrong.
- **$BEEF framing:** Would see it as "an AI that calls out bad projects." This is genuinely useful to them if they can understand the roasts.
- **Token behavior:** Least likely to buy based on entertainment alone. Might buy if the "protect yourself from scams" narrative is prominent.

### $BEEF Relevance

**Why they'd follow:** Accessibility + education. The best $BEEF roasts could teach them to spot scams in the format of a joke.

**Conversion barrier:** High. They need multiple touchpoints. First encounter is probably someone else's screenshot.

**Growth value:** Represents future full crypto participants. If $BEEF consistently creates content newbies can share with their non-crypto friends, it drives top-of-funnel growth.

**Risk:** Newbies are most likely to misinterpret aggressive roasting as an attack on all of crypto.

### Sample Reactions
